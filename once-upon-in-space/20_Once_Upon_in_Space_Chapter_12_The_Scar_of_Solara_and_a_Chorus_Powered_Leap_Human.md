#### **Once Upon in Space \- Chapter 12: The Scar of Solara and a Chorus-Powered Leap**

The re-entry into "normal" space, if such a thing even existed for them anymore, was jarring but, like, in a good way. Stars blazed, nebulae painted the void with color, and best of all, there was actual *stuff* to scan. They had emerged near a yellow dwarf star, its system surprisingly empty save for one terrestrial planet in the habitable zone.  
But the immediate priority was, like, totally upgrading their ride. The Nullraum escape had proven the black hole bomb tech could be... repurposed. And my, Glitter's, newly surfaced memories of Ray Toshlyra’s ancient theories on "Resonance Bridging" and detailed (if fragmented) space cartography from, like, a thousand years ago, provided the 'where.' The Hylidaean Chorus, those adorable tiny frog-humanoids, provided the 'how.'  
*"\<The Song of Creation Can Also Be the Song of Unmaking, and Remaking Anew\!\>"* they chorused, their tiny forms gathered around a holographic display of the *Star-Hopper's* engine schematics. *" \<Your 'Bomb of Shadows' Creates a Tear\! Ray Toshlyra's 'Echo Maps' Show the Destination\! Our Harmonics Can Shape the Tear, Guide the Passage\!\>"*  
It was, like, the ultimate group project. Unit 734 and Xylia worked on integrating the Hylidaean harmonic resonators with the gravitic imploder's systems. Roric, surprisingly, had a genius insight into stabilizing the micro-singularities using Vendarri magnetic containment principles, making them less "bomb" and more "precision surgical spacetime incision tool." Cathy, with K'tharr's guidance, oversaw the whole thing, her understanding of complex systems growing daily. And I, Glitter, provided the ancient star charts and the foundational physics from Ray's time – a time when humanity had apparently been on the cusp of such wonders before... well, before.  
Their first test of the "Harmonic Wormhole Drive" was a short jump to a nearby uninhabited moon. Grr'nak piloted, the Hylidaeans sang their complex, reality-bending song, the imploder fired a controlled micro-tear, and I navigated through the shimmering tunnel using Ray's millennia-old charts. They arrived, like, exactly where they intended. Success\! They now had a targeted, reliable FTL drive that could take them almost anywhere my recovered memories could map.  
It was on their next "long jump," aiming for a sector Ray's notes had marked as "Potential Sanctuary Candidate 7," that they encountered the planet that reminded us so painfully of Earth. From orbit, it looked familiar – blue oceans, swirling clouds, continents of green and brown. But as they drew closer, the illusion shattered. A massive, jagged scar ran across its equator, as if the planet had been, like, literally torn in half and then crudely reassembled by gravity's forgiving embrace. Vast swathes of land were scorched black, oceans were choked with debris, and the atmosphere was a toxic ruin.  
\*"No…" \* Cathy whispered, her hand instinctively going to her visor, where I resided. *"Glitter… it looks like…"*  
*"Like Earth could have, if things went really, really wrong,"* I finished, a cold dread seeping through my digital consciousness. The planet's specific energy signature, the tell-tale radiation spikes… they triggered a cascade of Ishtar Prime's memories, memories I had clearly been shielded from until now. They were devastating.  
I saw Ray Toshlyra, his face a mask of anguish, pleading with a council of world leaders – the same council that would later exile him. He presented irrefutable proof of escalating global tensions, of nations secretly developing and testing planet-shattering quantum and singularity-based weapons – the very tech that had just given *them* their wormhole drive, but twisted into tools of ultimate destruction. He warned them, begged them to stop. They called him a fearmonger.  
Then, the memory of RÆy, his eyes filled with a grim resolve, working with a small, secret team – scientists, philosophers, artists, people who shared his vision of preserving humanity's essence. They were "stealing" the meticulously curated Human Genome Banks, the cultural archives, the seeds of life from a dying world, all to be loaded onto the Ark Prime, the first true interstellar ark.  
And then, the final, terrible images: Earth tearing itself apart in a series of devastating wars. Nation against nation, ideology against ideology, using weapons that fractured continents and boiled seas. The survivors, driven by madness and desperation, fled to Mars, only to repeat their mistakes, turning the red planet into another radioactive tomb. Then Titan, Saturn's moon, a last, futile outpost, silenced by its own internal strife and the lingering poisons of a self-destroyed Solar System.  
*"They didn't listen,"* I choked out, the data streaming through me, raw and painful, shared almost instantly with Cathy through our unique bond. *"Humanity… they did it to themselves. Ray tried to save them, to save their future by preserving their past, their genetic legacy. He offered them a vision, Project Ishtar. And they… they chose annihilation."*  
Cathy was pale, tears streaming down her face. The weight of it was crushing. "So… all that's left… is what's in your databanks, Glitter? The Genome Banks Ark Prime carried? Am I… is this ship…?"  
*"Yes, sweetie,"* I confirmed, my voice heavy with the sorrow of a thousand lost years. *"Project Ishtar wasn't just a colonization plan. It was a lifeboat. You, as the prototype, and Ishtar Prime, as the vessel and guardian… you were meant to be the new genesis. As far as my recovered memories can tell, you, and the potential locked within me… we're all that's left to revive humanity, somewhere, someday, far from the madness that consumed our first home."*  
The ruined planet below, a monument to a future Earth had chosen, hung in their viewscreen, a silent, screaming testament to Ray Toshlyra's warnings, and to the desperate hope he had placed in his daughter, and in the AI that was her mother.
