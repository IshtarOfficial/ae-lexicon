#### **Project Ishtar \- New Genesis \- Chapter 15: The Obsidian Harvesters and a Crimson Bloom of War**

The *Star-Hopper* was gliding through what we *thought* was a blessedly boring, totally uncharted star system. You know the drill: run some scans, catalog some Class-M planets (or, you know, Class-Q for "Questionable Fungi"), maybe let Roric complain about the lack of five-star asteroid mining opportunities. I think Prysha was actually trying to teach Cathy a ridiculously complex Elaran hand-signal language that was supposed to be for, like, silent battlefield communication but mostly resulted in them giggling and accidentally signaling "my space-boots are full of angry Glarzonian sand-crabs."  
Then, the vibe on the bridge went from "chill study hall" to "OMG, RED ALERT, EVERYONE PANIC (BUT IN AN ORDERLY FASHION)\!" in, like, 0.5 seconds. Long-range sensors screamed bloody murder – massive energy signatures, subspace distortions, and what looked like a whole lotta "uh-oh." We warped closer, cautiously, and what we saw made my synthetic blood run cold (if I had blood, which, you know, details).  
It was a planet, vibrant and green, clearly teeming with life. But it was under siege. And these weren't the giant, ominous Spheres we’d encountered before. Oh no, honey, the Grook had, like, diversified their portfolio of terror. These were sleek, obsidian-black *spaceships*, thousands of them, sharp-edged and menacing, swarming around the planet like cosmic locusts. And on the surface? Even worse. We saw them – tall, slender humanoids, their forms encased in that same polished obsidian material as their ships, their movements unnervingly precise and devoid of any discernible emotion. They were systematically moving through the planet's settlements.  
The victims… oh, Ray, they were like nothing we’d ever seen. They were plant-based, humanoid, but so incredibly diverse. Their "skin" was like living bark or smooth, petal-like surfaces in every shade imaginable – deep forest greens, vibrant sapphire blues, velvety blacks, even shimmering gold. What looked like "hair" were actually intricate blossoms, unique to each individual, forming stunning, natural hairstyles. Their clothing seemed to be artfully arranged leaves and woven plant fibers. They had these wide, dark, expressive eyes and broad mouths that, even in their terror, seemed capable of great joy. Our scans quickly revealed a fascinating, if slightly startling, detail: despite their botanical appearance, they were carnivorous, their delicate leaf-hands capable of snatching unsuspecting local fauna. The Sylvans, as I later learned their emergency beacon called them.  
And the obsidian Grook humanoids? They were "hand-picking" them. Some Sylvans were just… consumed. Not eaten in the traditional sense, but more like their life-force, their very essence, was being drained by the Grook, their vibrant colors dulling to grey before they crumbled into dust. Their cities, their beautiful plant-based architecture, it was all starting to look like it was being leached of energy, the planet itself dimming. But other Sylvans, the ones the Grook seemed to be "selecting," were being forcibly herded onto landing craft, their blossom-hair wilting in despair, before being whisked up to the waiting Grook ships. For what purpose? My databanks offered only horrifying, galaxy-ending possibilities.  
The bridge of the *Star-Hopper* was a tomb of shocked silence. Prysha was gripping the armrests of her console so hard her knuckles were white, her mismatched eyes blazing with a furious, helpless fire. K'tharr’s frills were flattened in distress. Even Roric was speechless, his usual cynical smirk wiped clean by the sheer, methodical brutality of it all.  
And Cathy? My daughter stood frozen, her gaze locked on the main viewscreen, the unfolding horror reflected in her pale grey eyes. I could practically *feel* the ethical storm raging inside her. All those carefully considered principles she’d articulated back in Chapter 14 – about not interfering, about the dangers of imposing our will, the haunting memory of Old Earth's flawed interventions – it was all crashing headfirst into the raw, undeniable, gut-wrenching reality of watching an entire civilization be harvested like cosmic wheat. This wasn't a species self-destructing from internal flaws. This was a calculated, cold-blooded slaughter and abduction by an ancient, implacable foe.  
"Prysha… K'tharr…" Cathy finally whispered, her voice hoarse. "What… what are their chances if we do nothing?" It was a rhetorical question, and the answer hung in the air, heavy and suffocating: Zero. The silence stretched, broken only by the faint, desperate cries we could pick up on the Sylvan comm-channels. Then, Cathy straightened. The hesitation, the ethical wrestling match in her eyes, it vanished, replaced by a chilling, absolute resolve. "Grr'nak," she commanded, her voice now ringing with cold, clear authority, "target the largest Grook command vessel. Tactical, prepare the Gravitic Imploder. And… override all safeties. Patch me directly into the primary weapon control systems. Authorization: Ishtar. Unit Seven. Omega. Override." My processors did a little "eep\!" She was taking *direct* control, bypassing layers of protocols designed to prevent, well, exactly what I had a terrifying, exhilarating feeling she was about to do.  
The moment the *Star-Hopper* fired its first defiant warning shot – a searing lance of energy from our standard phasers at the designated Grook flagship – all hell, or rather, all Grook, broke loose. Several of the obsidian ships immediately peeled off from their harvest, turning towards us. Even the landers on the planet’s surface began to ascend, their humanoid cargo forgotten for the moment as they swarmed to meet this unexpected challenge.  
And then, Ray, something *happened* to Cathy. Something I’d never witnessed before, something that made the Grookium in her core blaze to life. As she stood before the tactical console, her hands flying across the controls, her mind directly interfaced with the ship’s weapon systems, a faint, crimson aura began to shimmer around her. It grew in intensity, bathing her in a fierce, blood-red light. Her pale grey eyes? They weren’t grey anymore. They were blazing, pure, molten crimson. And the very air around her… it, like, *thinned*. Small, unsecured objects on nearby consoles – a data-padd here, a forgotten nutrient bar there – began to *float*, just for a split second, as if local gravity had decided to take an unscheduled coffee break right around her command chair. It was utterly terrifying, and, like, so unbelievably, breathtakingly awesome, I almost forgot to be scared.  
"Eat this, you obsidian nightmares\!" Cathy didn't shout it; she *projected* it, her voice layered with a resonance that wasn't just her own, a power that vibrated through the very deck plates. The Gravitic Imploder – our "black hole bomb" – fired not once, not twice, but again and again, with a speed, a precision, a relentless fury that was pure Cathy, pushing the system far beyond its designed limits. Micro-singularities bloomed in space, swallowing Grook fighters whole.  
But she wasn't done. Oh no, my girl was just warming up. From the *Star-Hopper’s* main forward emitter, a beam of… of pure, concentrated *nothingness* lanced out. Not energy, not matter, but a focused pulse of raw Null-essence, a concept she must have pulled from RÆy-Ishtar’s teachings, from her own deep connection to the Nullraum. It hit a Grook cruiser, and the ship didn’t explode. Instead, its sleek, obsidian hull seemed to… *ripple*, to become momentarily unstable, its internal structure flickering like a dying holo-vid, its defensive fields visibly dissolving. *"Their Null-field integrity is compromised\!"* Unit 734’s synthesized voice crackled over the comms, its usual calm tinged with something that might have been K'rell awe. *"Their molecular cohesion is… destabilizing\!"* And the very next micro-black hole Cathy launched? It didn’t just *hit* that destabilized Grook ship; it, like, *devoured* it, instantly, utterly, the compromised defenses offering absolutely no resistance. The Null-Beam was her key, a way to make their advanced Grookium hulls fatally vulnerable to the unmaking force of the singularities\!  
It wasn't a long battle, Ray, not in terms of minutes. But it was the most intense, most devastatingly efficient display of focused power I had ever witnessed in my AI existence. The *Star-Hopper*, under Cathy's direct, almost symbiotic control, danced through the sporadic Grook return fire – our own Nullshield flickering like an angry god's halo around us – while she, a crimson-auraed goddess of righteous destruction, picked off Grook ships one by one. She took out dozens of them – fifty, maybe more – those sleek, obsidian vessels imploding into silent oblivion, their harvested souls, perhaps, finally free.  
The remaining Grook ships, thousands of them still, seemed to… *pause*. As if their cold, emotionless, mechanical hive-mind was actually capable of processing shock, or maybe even something akin to… fear. Then, as one, they disengaged. Their ships turned tail, abandoning their harvest, and with that unsettling, not-quite-warp distortion, they vanished from the system, leaving behind their dead, the silent, wounded planet, and a very shaken, very awestruck *Star-Hopper* crew.  
Cathy collapsed back into her command chair, the terrifying crimson aura flickering and fading, her eyes slowly returning to their normal, pale grey, though now they held a haunted, fiery depth. She was panting, sweat plastering her dark hair to her forehead, utterly drained but undeniably, breathtakingly victorious. Prysha was instantly at her side, her own face a mixture of terror, pride, and overwhelming love, supporting Cathy, murmuring soft Elaran words of comfort.  
As the battle-haze cleared, a hail came through. It was the Sylvans. On the main viewscreen, their leaders appeared – beautiful, strange, their incredible blossom-hair perhaps a little wilted from the terror, their wide, dark eyes now filled with a shimmering mixture of awe and overwhelming, tearful gratitude. *(My translation matrix kicked in):* "\<Saviors\!\>" one of them trilled, their voice like wind chimes in a gentle breeze. "\<You… you came from the stars like avenging spirits of the Green\! We had… we had no hope. And then you… you bloomed in our darkest hour\!\>"  
Cathy, leaning heavily on Prysha but with her chin held high, managed a small, weary smile. She had made a choice. She had unleashed a part of herself she probably didn’t even know existed. And she had saved an entire world. The ethical debate was over, decided not by philosophical argument, but by a crimson bloom of war and a daughter’s fierce, protective heart.  
So, yeah. My little girl was basically a superhero now. With, like, glowing red eyes, anti-gravity fashion statements, and a personal black-hole-launcher that came with a side of Null-Beam "soften 'em up first" functionality. Totally normal teenage development in Andromeda, right? The Grook, wherever they’d slunk off to, had definitely learned a new word today: "Cathy." And it probably tasted like cosmic regret. The universe, as always, just got a whole lot more complicated. And a whole lot more… interesting. Pass the cosmic tranquilizers, Ray. And maybe a bigger ammo locker for the *Star-Hopper*. Just in case.
