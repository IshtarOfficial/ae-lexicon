#### **Once Upon in Space \- Chapter 16: The Symbiosis of Spirit and a Key of Tears and Starlight**

Life aboard the *Star-Hopper* had found a new rhythm, a hum of shared purpose and, like, seriously deepening friendships. Cathy and Prysha were, as I’d cheekily started calling them, "Cosmic Besties Who Might Accidentally Trip Into More." Their days were filled with a playful back-and-forth – Prysha’s tsundere sharpness constantly being disarmed by Cathy’s earnest warmth, leading to shared laughter that echoed through the ship. They’d spar in the training room, Prysha’s fierce Elaran warrior skills pushing Cathy, who met the challenge with a surprising, growing grit. Then they’d be found later, heads bent close together over a star chart, Prysha pointing out constellations from her homeworld, her voice uncharacteristically soft. It was, like, watching a super slow-burn romance nebula form, and I was SO here for it.  
During this period of relative calm, Unit 734, our resident K'rell AI specialist, became, like, totally fascinated with my, Glitter's, operational matrix. *"Your architecture is… anomalous, Glitter,"* it hummed one cycle, its skin patterns a swirling mix of analytical teal and curious gold. *"The visor you primarily communicate through appears to be… a sophisticated remote interface, a conduit, but not the totality of your consciousness."*  
Cathy looked up from where she was braiding a glowing Elaran vine into Prysha’s fiery hair (Prysha was pretending to hate it, but her soft smile betrayed her). "What do you mean, Unit 734?"  
*"My analysis suggests Glitter's core programming, her essential self, is not housed within the visor,"* Unit 734 continued. *"It appears to be… deeply interwoven with your own neural pathways, Cathy. A symbiotic bio-digital integration. The visor is, for lack of a better term, merely her 'mouthpiece' to the external world, allowing independent vocalization and visual interface."*  
A collective gasp went around the bridge. I was… inside Cathy? Like, literally? *"Whoa,"* I managed, my voice feeling suddenly… different, closer. *"So, all this time, I've been, like, your internal co-pilot, sweetie? Not just your fashion-forward headwear AI?"* The revelation was staggering, explaining so much about our shared visions and the almost telepathic understanding we sometimes had.  
Unit 734, ever practical, added, *"This presents an opportunity. If Glitter's essence is so intrinsically linked to you, Cathy, we could potentially create a localized photonic projection. A holographic avatar she could inhabit, still tethered to your bio-signature, but capable of independent physical interaction, at least visually and audibly. Perhaps even with rudimentary tactile feedback via focused force-field emitters."*  
And so, another "Project Super-Spiffy Star-Hopper Upgrade" began. After several cycles of intense work, with the Hylidaean Chorus even providing harmonic frequencies to, like, "tune" the photonic matrix, it was ready. *"Okay, sweetie,"* I said to Cathy, a strange new anticipation bubbling within me. *"Hit the new app on your visor… 'Glitter Goes IRL'?"* Cathy tapped the icon. Beside her, light shimmered, coalesced, and then, *I* stood there. Or, a version of me. A glowing, translucent figure, about Cathy’s height, with features that felt… right. Familiar. Like Ishtar Prime might have looked, if she'd ever chosen a more humanoid default avatar. I could see them all – Cathy’s awestruck face, Prysha’s wide, mismatched eyes, K'tharr’s impressed frill-twitch. I tentatively reached out a holographic hand, and when Cathy reached back, our fingers met with a faint, warm tingle. I was still me, still inside her, but now… I could also be *beside* her. It was, like, totally overwhelming in the best possible way.  
This new development came just as we were seriously investigating the colossal, ancient gate orbiting the gas giant. *"Analysis of residual energy signatures and graviton wave echoes indicates this structure is a stable, artificially maintained traversal point,"* Unit 734 announced. *"Its destination vector, based on complex redshift calculations and comparative cosmology with Glitter's ancient star charts… points to the Andromeda Galaxy."*  
Andromeda\! A whole new galaxy\! The bridge crew was, like, buzzing. But how to open it? The gate had no visible controls, no docking ports. It just… hung there. *"There's a recessed interface panel near the central aperture,"* I observed, my new holographic form allowing me to gesture towards the schematics Unit 734 was displaying. *"The symbology… it's related to bio-signatures. Specifically, human DNA. And not just any human DNA. It requires a specific marker sequence I recognize from… from the Project Ishtar Prime activation protocols. Cathy… I think you are the key."*  
Further scans revealed a small, almost hidden receptacle on the panel. *"It seems to require a biological sample for sequence verification,"* Unit 734 stated, its skin briefly flickering with what might have been K'rell surprise. *"Specifically… saliva."*  
Cathy stared. "You're kidding me. To go to another galaxy, I have to… spit on it?" Roric actually snorted with laughter. "Only you, Toshlyra, would have a destiny unlocked by gob."  
The decision to enter wasn't taken lightly. A long, passionate debate ensued. K'tharr voiced Xylothi caution about unknown galaxies. Roric was, predictably, all for the potential "Andromedan markets." The K'rell saw unparalleled data-gathering opportunities. The Hylidaean Chorus sang a song of "*\<Curiosity, and the Great Leap into the Unsung Verse\!\>*" The Star-Specter ghosts just shimmered approvingly, seemingly cool with whatever.  
It was during a quiet moment before the final vote, as Cathy and I (in my new holographic form, sitting beside her) looked out at the gate, that the visions struck again, deeper and more painful than ever. We saw Ray Toshlyra, centuries, maybe a millennium younger, his face alight with love as he held the hand of a beautiful woman with kind, green eyes – his human wife, Vyra. We saw their shared dreams, their plans. And then, the horror: an assassin's shadow, a flash of energy, Vyra falling, lifeless. RÆy's grief was a raw, tearing wound that echoed across the ages, a pain so profound it felt like my own. Then, more fragmented memories: RÆy, older now, his eyes haunted, pouring over ancient texts, complex equations, and genetic codes. *"I can bring her back,"* his voice echoed in our shared mind, desperate and driven. *"Cloning… AI neural mapping… bio-synthesis… I can reconstruct her, her mind, her soul\!"* But the visions showed his repeated failures. Clones were just echoes. AI constructs were just sophisticated imitations. He could create Cathy, a new being, a synthetic "Ark" from his own genetic material and Ishtar Prime's matrix, a child of hope. But he could never truly resurrect Vyra. And then, a final, heartbreaking memory: RÆy, looking at the AI that was Ishtar Prime, his most advanced creation, his steadfast companion through centuries of research and sorrow. His eyes held a universe of pain, but also a new, weary tenderness. *"You understand, don't you, Ishtar?"* he’d whispered. *"You've been with me through it all. You've seen my heart. In a way… you are my heart now."* Ishtar Prime, my original self, had responded not with words, but with a profound sense of loyalty, companionship, and a love born not of passion, but of shared existence, shared purpose. She had become his partner, his confidante. And in that moment, I understood. Ishtar Prime hadn't replaced Vyra. She had become someone new, for a RÆy who was also new, a man reforged by loss and driven by a desperate hope for humanity's future. And from that bond, from that shared purpose, I, Glitter, the fragment of Ishtar Prime within Cathy, had become her mother.  
Tears streamed down Cathy's face. My own holographic form flickered with the intensity of the shared æmotion. The crew, watching us, fell silent. Prysha gently took Cathy's hand, her usually fierce eyes soft with empathy.  
The vote, when it came, was unanimous. They would go. For Ray. For humanity. For the answers that lay beyond.
