#### **Once Upon in Space \- Chapter 9: The Choir of Frogs and the Cosmic Question Mark**

After several weeks spent on Paradise Found – recalibrating, resupplying, and, like, emotionally processing our collective cosmic baggage – the *Star-Hopper* was ready. The visions of Ray, though unsettling, had given us a new sense of urgency. Unit 734, cross-referencing the vague locations from my visions with Ray's last known research into "anomalous stellar phenomena," had plotted a new, tentative course.  
Many light-years later, after navigating a particularly tricky asteroid field (where Grr'nak's piloting and Roric's uncanny ability to spot valuable ore veins, even in worthless rock, both proved invaluable) and a minor diplomatic incident with a species that communicated entirely through interpretive dance (K'tharr, surprisingly, was a natural), we found ourselves drawn towards an unusual energy signature.  
It was a tiny starship, impossibly small, like a child's toy lost in the vastness of space. It zipped and darted with an agility that defied its apparent simplicity.  
*"Okay, that's new,"* I commented, zooming in with our sensors. *"Looks like a… well, it looks like a highly polished space-pebble with fins. And it's, like, hailing us… musically?"*  
Indeed, the tiny ship was emitting a complex series of harmonic tones. On the viewscreen, the inhabitants appeared: tiny, humanoid figures, no bigger than Cathy's hand, with smooth, moist-looking skin in shades of green and brown, large golden eyes, and wide, expressive mouths. They looked, for all the worlds, like adorable, bipedal frogs.  
And then they "spoke." Not individually, but as one. Their voices, dozens of them, blended into a perfectly harmonized, multi-layered choir.  
*"\<Greetings, Travelers of the Woven Ship\!\>"* the choir sang, the translation appearing as glowing text on our screen, courtesy of Unit 734's rapid linguistic analysis. *"\<We are the Hylidaean Chorus. We have Sensed Your Quest, the Echo of the Lost Architect\!\>"*  
Cathy exchanged a look with K'tharr. The "Lost Architect" – that had to be Ray.  
*"\<The Path to Answers is Not Always Straight\!\>"* the Hylidaeans chorused, their tiny ship executing a series of joyful loops. *"\<A Rare Confluence Opens\! A Passage Through Folds of Space-Time\! Will You Follow Our Song to the Threshold?\>"*  
Before us, space began to… ripple. Like water disturbed by an unseen hand. And then, a swirling vortex of impossible colors, a tunnel through the fabric of reality itself, tore open: a wormhole. The tiny Hylidaean ship zipped towards it, pausing at its Maw, their collective voices echoing with an invitation.  
*"\<The Universe Offers a Choice\! Quick Now, Before the Song Fades and the Passage Closes\!\>"*  
The bridge of the *Star-Hopper* was silent for a beat. K'tharr's frills were fully extended, a sign of intense concentration. Xylia was already running sensor scans on the wormhole, her purple frills a blur. Unit 734 and its K'rell colleagues were, I could tell, processing probabilities at, like, light speed, their skin patterns a mesmerizing, unreadable dance. Roric just looked, like, totally gobsmacked.  
Cathy looked at me, her eyes asking the unspoken question. My visions of Ray, alive but changed, burned in my memory. This wormhole… it felt like a gamble, a leap into the unknown. But wasn't their whole quest just that?  
*"Well, team,"* I said, my voice echoing the uncertainty and the wild, improbable hope that filled the bridge. *"Looks like we have a decision to make. Do we follow the singing space-frogs into the pretty, possibly deadly, interdimensional whirlpool? Or do we, like, take the scenic route?"*  
The fate of their search, the answers to everything, might just lie on the other side. Or, you know, cosmic annihilation. No pressure.
