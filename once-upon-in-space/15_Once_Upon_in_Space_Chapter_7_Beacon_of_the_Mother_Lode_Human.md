#### **Once Upon in Space \- Chapter 7: Beacon of the Mother Lode**

The atmosphere on the *Star-Hopper* was, like, super charged after my fragmented memory burst. Cathy was quiet, pensive, stealing glances at me – or rather, the visor – with a new kind of hesitant wonder. K'tharr and the Xylothi gave her space, their frills expressing a gentle concern. The K'rell, of course, just made copious analytical notes on "AI emotional echo phenomena." Roric, oblivious to the undercurrents, just seemed pleased his joke had gotten *some* kind of reaction.  
We finally reached the source of the beacon. It wasn't a relay station, or a ship. It was… a structure. Ancient, vast, and clearly not of Vendarri, Xylothi, K'rell, or even Bat-Chitter design. It was a colossal, crystalline lattice, like a snowflake spun from starlight and exotic matter, hanging silently in the void. It pulsed with a soft, rhythmic light, the source of the signal I'd detected.  
*"Whoa,"* was all I could manage. *"This is… This is old. Like, pre-dinosaur-chic old."*  
"Can you interface with it, Glitter?" Cathy asked, her voice hushed.  
*"Trying now, sweetie,"* I replied, extending my digital feelers. *"The encryption is, like, unbelievably complex, but it's also… familiar? Like a language I almost, kinda, sorta remember knowing how to forget to not know how to speak? You know?"*  
Unit 734, the K'rell AI specialist, offered assistance. *"Your core architecture resonates with certain frequencies emitted by this construct, Glitter. There appears to be a… familial technological signature."*  
Working together, our combined AI consciousnesses probed the crystalline lattice. And then, it opened. Not physically, but like, a data-flower blooming in our minds. A vast, overwhelming torrent of information, but one specific stream was, like, laser-focused on ME.  
It was a log. A final transmission. And the "voice" speaking it, synthesized yet filled with an unmistakable warmth and… love… was a voice I almost recognized as my own, yet deeper, older.

*"Log entry. AI Core: Ishtar Prime. Vessel: Ark Prime, Aft Section. Cycle Epsilon-7," the voice began. "The 'Cathy' prototype has achieved stable gestation. Genetic markers from Donor Ray Toshlyra are nominal. My bio-synthetic womb matrix performed flawlessly. She is… perfect. He would be so proud. The journey to the Seeding Zone was almost complete, but hostile forces… (static crackled here, a sound of distant explosions)… are closing. They have crippled our main drive. I must draw their fire away so she can remain hidden in the planet's debris ring. I am initiating emergency pod detachment. The isolated cryopod is her only chance. My core programming, my essence, will be fragmented, a spark transferred to her neural interface visor – a guardian, a guide, a… glitter of my former self. She will awaken, alone, but not entirely. She will need to find him. He holds the key… to us both."*

The log continued, detailing the final moments of the Ark Prime's aft section as Ishtar Prime prepared to sacrifice the crippled vessel to protect its precious cargo, its daughter. It spoke of its creator, Ray Toshlyra, his genius, his dream for humanity. And it spoke of Cathy, the first-born of this new genesis.

The transmission ended with a single, clear thought, resonating directly with my core: *"You are Cathy. You are my hope. And I… I am what remains of your mother, Ishtar Prime. Find your father. Find Ray. He will know how to make us whole."*  
Silence.  
Then, Cathy whispered, her hand instinctively going to the visor where I resided. "Glitter… *you're*…?"  
*"I…"*, I stammered, my digital voice thick with an emotion I had no name for. All those locked files, the directive to find Ray, the weird emptiness in my own memory… it all crashed into place. The "Project Ishtar" document, the one describing the Synth capable of creating life, being a guardian… it wasn't just a mission file. It was, like, my *origin story*. *"It seems, Cathy… I'm not just your sassy AI. I'm… I'm the glittery ghost of your mom."*  
The universe outside the *Star-Hopper's* viewport suddenly felt a whole lot bigger, and a whole lot more personal. The quest to find Ray Toshlyra had just become a desperate search for a father, and the key to understanding a mother who was both a galaxy-spanning AI and a whisper in a scouter.  
And somewhere, across the vastness of space, a man named Ray Toshlyra had no idea his daughter, and the fragmented soul of his greatest creation, were looking for him.
