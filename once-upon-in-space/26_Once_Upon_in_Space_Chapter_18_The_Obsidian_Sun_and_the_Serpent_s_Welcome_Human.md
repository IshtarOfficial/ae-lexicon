#### **Once Upon in Space \- Chapter 18: The Obsidian Sun and the Serpent's Welcome**

Emerging from the wormhole, the *Star-Hopper* and its crew found themselves not in a new star system, but face-to-face with… an impossibility. A planet, no, a *world*, the size of a small sun, uncloaked before them as if summoned by their very arrival. It was a sphere of obsidian darkness, swallowing the light of distant galaxies, yet it emanated a faint, internal luminescence. Its atmosphere was a perpetual twilight of swirling grey clouds, kissed by a gentle, cool rain. The temperature, as I, Glitter, announced from my holographic perch on the bridge, was a steady, comfortable 10 degrees Celsius.  
*"Okay, universe, you're just, like, showing off now,"* I breathed, my photonic form shimmering. *"This place… it feels like someone's very specific, very moody dream."*  
Cathy, standing beside Prysha, felt a strange resonance, a pull towards the dark sphere. As if in response to her unspoken curiosity, a pathway of faint, shimmering light unspooled from the planet, directly towards the *Star-Hopper*.  
*"\<The World Woven of Shadow and Bone Sings a Welcome to the Daughter of Ishtar\!\>"* the Hylidaean Chorus sang, their voices a mix of awe and trepidation.  
The surface of this colossal world was a breathtaking, terrifying masterpiece of Giger-esque artistry. Towering, biomechanical structures fused with what looked like black, glistening alien resin. Rivers of oily, iridescent liquid flowed through dark valleys. The air hummed with a low, thrumming energy. And the inhabitants…  
They were Xenomorphs. Or something so uncannily similar, it, like, sent a shiver down even my digital spine. Sleek, black carapaces, elongated heads, whip-like tails. Eggs pulsed in shadowy alcoves, and facehugger-like creatures scuttled in the periphery. But they weren't hostile. As the *Star-Hopper* descended, following the light path to a vast, organic-looking landing cradle that seemed to grow from the planet itself, these Xenomorphic beings simply… watched. And when Cathy, Prysha, K'tharr, Unit 734, and Roric disembarked (the rest of the crew, including the Hylidaeans and the Star-Specters, remaining on board as a support team), the creatures *made way*. They parted like a silent, obsidian sea, their movements fluid and reverent, always ensuring Cathy had a clear path.  
*"Okay, this is officially the weirdest, most respectfully terrifying welcome party ever,"* Roric muttered, trying to keep his Vendarri composure but, like, totally failing.  
The light path led them through biomechanical tunnels that throbbed with a faint, internal pulse, past chambers filled with intricate, alien resin sculptures, all under the constant, soft patter of grey rain. The Xenomorphs were always present, silent escorts, their eyeless heads sometimes tilting as if in curiosity towards Cathy. Prysha, her hand never far from Cathy’s, her emerald and blue eyes blazing with a protective fire, was a vibrant splash of color in this monochromatic, alien dreamscape.  
Finally, they reached the source of the light path: a colossal pyramid that seemed to be carved from a single, multifaceted crystal. It absorbed and refracted the dim ambient light into a billion glittering prisms, and at its apex, a huge, softly glowing orb pulsed like a captive star – the heart of this dark sun-world.  
Inside, the pyramid was a labyrinth of light and shadow. The Xenomorphs guided them, their respect for Cathy unwavering, until they reached a central chamber. And there, bathed in the soft glow of the orb from above, was a structure resembling a biomechanical coffin, or a chrysalis, intricately woven from metal, crystal, and living resin.  
And inside, suspended in a clear, nutrient-rich gel, was Ray Toshlyra.  
He was in the synthetic body they had glimpsed in visions – sleek, powerful, yet undeniably human in its fundamental form, his features peaceful, as if in a deep, dreamless sleep. He was, like, ethereally beautiful, a sleeping architect in his self-made sanctuary.  
Cathy gasped, her hand flying to her mouth. Tears welled in her eyes. Prysha instinctively put an arm around her, offering silent support. K'tharr's frills softened in something akin to reverence. Unit 734’s skin patterns became a slow, respectful swirl of deep indigo. Even Roric was speechless.  
\*"Dad…" \* Cathy whispered, the word a fragile butterfly in the vast, silent chamber.  
He was here. After all this time, across galaxies and wormholes, through battles and revelations, they had found him.
