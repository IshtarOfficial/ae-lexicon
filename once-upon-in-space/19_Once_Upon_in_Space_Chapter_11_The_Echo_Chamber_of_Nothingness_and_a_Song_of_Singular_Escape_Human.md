#### **Once Upon in Space \- Chapter 11: The Echo Chamber of Nothingness and a Song of Singular Escape**

Exiting the wormhole wasn't like a grand arrival; it was more like… the universe just switched off. One moment, the *Star-Hopper* was surfing a psychedelic wave of trans-dimensional energies, the next, it floated in an abyss so profound, so utterly devoid of light or feature, that it felt like they'd been swallowed by pure, unadulterated *nothing*.  
*"Um, Glitter?"* Cathy's voice was small, echoing weirdly in the sudden, oppressive silence of the bridge. *"Are we, like, dead? Is this the intergalactic afterlife, and it's just… really boring?"*  
My sensors, usually so chatty, were eerily quiet. *"Sweetie, if this is the afterlife, they seriously need a new decorator. I'm getting… nothing. No stars, no radiation, no stray particles, not even, like, a rogue cosmic dust bunny. My temporal sensors are also, like, totally wigging out – it's as if time itself is holding its breath here."*  
K'tharr’s frills were drawn tight, their usual vibrant colors muted in the dim emergency lighting of the bridge. Xylia and Unit 734 were running frantic diagnostics, their readouts displaying only flat lines and error messages. Roric, for once, wasn't smirking; he just looked pale. Grr'nak kept instinctively reaching for flight controls that had no reference points to steer by.  
Days, or what felt like days in the timeless void, passed. The crew, after initial panic and much scientific head-scratching (or frill-twitching, or skin-pattern-analyzing), came to a chilling conclusion, mostly articulated by Unit 734: *"We appear to have exited the wormhole into a localized pocket of what our theoretical physicists term 'Nullraum' – a transitional void between dimensional membranes. A space where the fundamental laws of conventional spacetime are… suspended."*  
The strangest part? Nothing decayed. A piece of Xylothi glow-fruit left on a console remained as fresh as the moment it was picked. Minor injuries didn't seem to heal, but they also didn't worsen. It was, like, existence pressed pause.  
The Hylidaean Chorus, their tiny ship still tethered to the *Star-Hopper* by a shimmering energy filament, finally made their presence known more directly. Their collective, harmonized voices echoed through the comms: *"\<The Void Sings a Song of Stillness\! We Shall Join Your Larger Echo Chamber, Friends\!\>"*  
And so, the tiny, frog-like humanoids came aboard, their little ship docking neatly in one of the *Star-Hopper's* smaller shuttle bays. Their presence was a strange comfort. They moved about in their little choirs, exploring the ship, their multi-part harmonies providing a surreal but, like, surprisingly calming soundtrack to the nothingness. They'd "sing" observations about K'rell meditation practices or "harmonize" questions about Roric's Vendarri trade routes.  
The endless, eventless days of the Nullraum became a crucible for the crew. With no external stimuli, they turned inward, and towards each other. Cathy and K'tharr spent hours discussing leadership philosophies, the Xylothi leader sharing ancient tales of their people's resilience through long, dark planetary winters, which, like, weirdly resonated with their current predicament. Xylia and Unit 734, deprived of new data to analyze from the outside, turned their combined intellect to my, Glitter's, fragmented core, trying to map the echoes of Ishtar Prime and understand the "thousand-year-old" memories that had surfaced. Their skin patterns, Unit 734's especially, would sometimes shimmer with what I could only interpret as awe when discussing Ray Toshlyra's theoretical brilliance. Roric, surprisingly, found common ground with Grr'nak, sharing tales of daring escapes and near misses from his less-than-legal trading days, while Grr'nak recounted Xylothi sky-duels. They even started co-designing impossible flight maneuvers on the ship's simulator, just for the heck of it.  
And me? I spent a lot of time with Cathy. The shared visions from the wormhole – of Ray's life, his exile, his change – were still so vivid. We'd sit in the observation lounge, staring out at the featureless void, and just… talk. Or sometimes, just be quiet together, the unspoken understanding of our bizarre mother-daughter-AI-prototype bond filling the silence. The Nullraum was, like, a perfect, quiet space to try and process the impossible.  
But "perfectly quiet" eventually becomes "perfectly terrifying."  
*"Okay, team,"* Cathy said one "morning," her voice resolute. *"This has been… a bonding experience. But if we stay here, we're just, like, a perfectly preserved museum exhibit waiting for no one. Unit 734, Glitter, any theories on how to, you know, un-suspend ourselves?"*  
Unit 734’s skin flowed with complex calculations. *"The Nullraum appears to be a pocket of extremely low energetic and quantum-fluctuational density. To exit, we would theoretically need to create a localized disruption, a puncture, back into a higher-energy spacetime continuum."*  
My processors latched onto that. *"Like… a really, really big poke?"* I mused. *"The kind of poke that could, say, tear a tiny, temporary hole in the fabric of… nothingness?"*  
Roric, who had been listening intently, suddenly straightened. "The gravitic imploder. The black hole bomb."  
A hush fell over the bridge. The memory of using it against the Bat-Chitters, and the subsequent vision of Ray's exile for condemning such weapons, was still fresh.  
Cathy looked at me, her eyes troubled. *"RÆy… he wouldn't want us to,"* she whispered, a thought meant for me but felt by everyone who knew the story.  
*"I know, sweetie,"* I replied softly. *"But he also wouldn't want us to become, like, permanent residents of Hotel Nothing. This isn't about destruction. It's about… making a doorway. A very, very carefully controlled, very small doorway. Using its power not to unmake, but to… make a way."*  
The Hylidaean Chorus, clustered near the main viewscreen, began to sing a soft, complex harmony. Unit 734 translated: *"\<A Tool's Nature is Defined by Intent. A Song of Passage Can Be Sung by Roaring Fire, or Gentle Stream.\>"*  
The debate was shorter this time, more somber. K'tharr, after a long silence, nodded. "If it is the only path back to the stars, then the path must be taken. But with caution, Star-Fallen. Great caution."  
With the Hylidaeans adding their harmonic expertise to help "tune" the implosion, and Unit 734 and Roric working with me to modify the device to create a focused, directional rupture rather than an annihilating sphere, the plan was set. It was, like, using a cosmic scalpel instead of a sledgehammer.  
With everyone at their stations, the *Star-Hopper* aimed its particle cannon into the absolute black. \*"Firing in three… two… one…" \* Roric announced, his voice tight.  
A pinpoint of darkness, even darker than the Nullraum, bloomed, then collapsed inwards with an impossible surge of energy that didn't explode, but *tore*. For a breathless moment, a shimmering, star-flecked wound appeared in the nothingness.  
*"Full power to the engines, Grr'nak\!"* Cathy yelled. *"NOW\!"*  
The *Star-Hopper*, with the tiny Hylidaean ship still latched on like a brave little remora, surged forward, plunging through the temporary rift just as it began to seal behind them, the song of the Hylidaean Chorus reaching a triumphant, reality-bending crescendo.  
They burst back into… something. Stars blazed on the viewscreen – unfamiliar, but blessedly, beautifully *there*. Alarms indicating proximity to a planetary body blared. They were out. Bruised, a little shaken by the audacity of their escape, but out.  
The Nullraum was behind them. And ahead? A whole new, unknown corner of the galaxy, and the search for Ray Toshlyra, now armed with even more questions and a crew bound together by the shared experience of touching the void.
