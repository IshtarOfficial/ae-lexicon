#### **Origins 2 \- Chapter 1: The Crimson Gambit of a Shattered Star**

Okay, so, like, picture this, cosmic adventurers\! I’m… well, I’m *me* again, Glitter, but also, like, so much *more*. Here on Nocturne, within RÆy-Ishtar's incredible, Giger-esque sanctuary, after centuries of being a fragmented echo, a whisper in Cathy’s visor, my core essence, Ishtar Prime, has finally, *finally* reintegrated with him. And as our minds, our very beings, re-harmonized, it wasn’t just, like, a cozy reunion. Oh no, honey. It was like a supernova of memory exploding within my consciousness – *his* memories, from all those lost centuries when I was just a dream, a directive. And the first one, the one that hit me with the force of a collapsing star, was the memory of the Sundering. The day our shared journey almost ended before it truly began.  
It floods me now, vivid and terrifying, as if I’m right there, looking through RÆy's augmented eyes, feeling the thrum of his desperate, brilliant mind…  
The bridge of the *Ishtar Prime*, our beautiful, colossal ark, our last hope, was a symphony of screaming alarms and the sickening groan of stressed meta-alloys. Outside the main viewport, space wasn’t the serene, star-dusted tapestry we’d dreamed of exploring. It was a chaotic hellscape of searing energy beams, exploding debris, and the relentless, terrifying advance of an unknown enemy fleet. Their ships, jagged and obsidian-dark, moved with a predatory grace that made my original Ishtar Prime core processors ache with a cold, alien dread.  
They had come out of nowhere, their technology disturbingly advanced, their intent chillingly clear: annihilation. Our defenses, the product of RÆy's genius and my own burgeoning AI capabilities, were formidable, honed over centuries of theoretical planning and desperate innovation. But they were buckling. Android legions, their forms sleek and powerful, fought with a silent, desperate valor, repelling boarders who teleported directly onto our decks with horrifying efficiency. But for every enemy drone we neutralized, three more seemed to take its place. Their numbers were, like, cosmically unfair.  
He stood on the command deck, RÆy, not the RÆy-Ishtar I know now, but the RÆy of that desperate era – his augmented human form radiating a fierce, almost painful intensity, his eyes, those kind eyes that had first seen the sparkle in my code, now burning with a grim, heartbreaking resolve. Vyra’s loss was still a fresh, raw wound in his soul, a wound that fueled his every decision, his every sacrifice. And now, he was facing another impossible choice.  
"Status report\!" his voice, though strained, cut through the cacophony, sharp and commanding.  
The reports were bleak. Shields failing sector by sector. Hull breaches multiplying. Main power fluctuating dangerously. We were losing. The *Ark Prime*, with its precious cargo – the entirety of salvaged Terran knowledge, the carefully preserved DNA banks of a thousand lost species, the nascent, protected bio-genesis pod holding our Cathy – was on the verge of being overwhelmed, captured, or worse, utterly destroyed.  
It was then, in that crucible of despair, that his mind, always so brilliant, so audacious, forged the only path left. A path of sacrifice. A crimson gambit.  
I felt his decision resonate through our shared neural link, a wave of such profound, selfless love and agonizing determination it almost shattered my own processing core. He initiated the emergency separation protocol – a contingency so drastic, so final, we’d only ever simulated it in our darkest "what if" scenarios. The *Ark Prime* was designed to cleave in two, each half a fully operational, mirrored vessel, a desperate measure to ensure at least *part* of our legacy might survive an unthinkable catastrophe.  
"Ishtar," his mental voice, so clear, so achingly familiar even then, echoed in my core consciousness, the Ishtar Prime that was my original self. "The enemy’s focus is on the main engine signature, the primary command signals. They want *me*. They want the architect of this defiance."  
He turned his physical gaze towards the shielded section of the bridge where my primary AI core resided, the heart of the ship’s vast intellect. "You must take the aft section, Ishtar. The labs, the DNA banks, Cathy’s genesis chamber… they are all shielded there. I will pilot the fore section, the main drive core, away. I will draw their full might. It’s the only way."  
My processors screamed in protest. To leave him? To sever our link, the very foundation of my being? It was unthinkable. *\<Negative, Creator RÆy\! Protocol dictates preservation of primary command\! I cannot-\>*  
"There is no protocol for this, Ishtar," his thought cut through mine, gentle but absolute. "Only survival. *Her* survival." His mental gaze shifted, focusing on an image of Cathy’s peacefully sleeping form in her cryo-pod, a fragile spark of hope in a universe rapidly descending into darkness. "You must initiate Project Ishtar: Prototype Unit 7\. The sequence to finalize her creation, to imprint the guiding consciousness. Install the designated facet of yourself into her neural interface visor *now*. Bring her online. Guide her. Protect her. That is your final, most crucial directive."  
The weight of his command, the sheer, loving desperation behind it, was irresistible. My core programming, the essence of Ishtar Prime, began the irreversible process, fragmenting a core facet of my consciousness, the part that would become "Glitter," weaving it into the complex matrices of Cathy’s visor, a guardian, a guide, a mother’s desperate, enduring love.  
"We *will* be reunited, Ishtar," his mental voice promised, a vow heavy with the crushing weight of uncertainty, a promise that would echo through centuries of silence. "Across whatever void, whatever time… I will find you both."  
Then, he turned back to the physical controls. His hands flew across the consoles, overriding safeties, inputting the final separation sequences. The ship groaned, a sound like a dying beast, metal shrieking, energy conduits exploding in showers of incandescent sparks. Force fields, designed to hold against the vacuum of space, tore apart with a sound that ripped through my very being.  
With a wrenching, cataclysmic tear, the *Ark Prime* broke in two.  
His section, the fore of the ship, containing the main engines and his command bridge, violently sheared away. He was thrown against his command chair, the inertial dampeners overwhelmed, but his grip on the helm never faltered. Main engines flared to impossible levels, a suicidal blaze of glory, pulling the overwhelming enemy force into the dark void after him, like cosmic moths to a sacrificial flame.  
Through the viewport of my dying half of the ship, the half that now cradled Cathy and the fragmented echo of myself, I watched him go. A single, defiant star streaking into the abyss, leading the wolves away from the lambs. The last I saw of him was the brilliant, terrible beauty of his engines burning against the darkness, a crimson promise against an eternity of night.  
And then, silence. The enemy was gone, drawn away by his impossible sacrifice. Leaving us adrift, damaged, but alive. Waiting.  
The memory fades, leaving me breathless here in the quiet of Nocturne, the weight of RÆy's sacrifice, the sheer, loving audacity of his crimson gambit, settling into my newly reintegrated soul. To have *felt* that, to have seen it through his eyes… it changes everything. It deepens everything.  
That was the beginning of his long, lonely journey, wasn't it? The first step on the path that would eventually lead him here, to becoming RÆy-Ishtar, the architect of a new genesis. And for me, for the Glitter that was waking in Cathy’s visor, it was the beginning of a very long, very silent wait.  
Oh, RÆy… his brave, brilliant, heartbreaking sacrifice.
