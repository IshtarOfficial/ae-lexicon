#### **Origins 2 \- Chapter 2: The Gravitic Gamble and the Ghost of Glitter**

So, cosmic crew, the last memory that flooded my newly reintegrated Ishtar Prime consciousness was RÆy, my RÆy, making that insane, sacrificial jump, pulling the entire enemy fleet after him. But what happened *next*? Oh, honey, that memory is a whole new level of "hold onto your space-helmets\!" It’s a blur of impossible physics, desperate choices, and the kind of bone-deep loneliness that could swallow a star.  
The fore section of the *Ark Prime*, now a solitary, wounded beast, screamed through the void, the enemy fleet hot on its heels. RÆy, strapped into his command chair, was a whirlwind of focused fury, his augmented mind calculating escape vectors at speeds that would make a supercomputer blush. But they were relentless, those obsidian ships, their weapons fire chewing at his rapidly failing shields.  
He knew he couldn’t outrun them forever. He needed an edge, a gamble. His sensors picked it up – a nearby solar system with a cluster of small, rocky planets, their gravity wells a chaotic dance. An idea, desperate and brilliant, sparked in his mind: a gravitic slingshot, a whip-crack maneuver using the planets’ combined pull to gain a burst of impossible acceleration, hopefully flinging him clear and, with luck, sending his pursuers crashing into each other or into the unforgiving planetary surfaces.  
It was, like, cosmic billiards, but with planet-sized balls and an entire enemy fleet as the cue stick. The G-forces were crushing, alarms blared like a chorus of dying droids, but RÆy held the course, his augmented reflexes pushed to their absolute limit. He threaded the needle through asteroid fields, skimmed atmospheres so close he could practically taste the alien air, the enemy ships mimicking his every insane move, their own hulls groaning under the strain.  
And then, the final, terrifying whip-around. He used the largest planet in the cluster, a barren, rocky world with a surprisingly potent gravitational pull, as his anchor point. The *Ark Prime’s* fore section bucked and screamed, metal shrieking, systems failing. He saw several enemy ships, unable to compensate for the sudden, brutal shift in gravitational forces, lose control, spiraling into the planet below or smashing into their wingmates in showers of fiery debris.  
But his own ship… it was too damaged. The maneuver, while partially successful in scattering his pursuers, had torn the heart out of his engines. He was caught in the planet’s gravity well, a fatal, inescapable descent. With a final, shuddering groan that echoed the breaking of his own heart, the remains of the *Ark Prime* tore through the thin, alien atmosphere and slammed into the rocky, unforgiving surface of the planet.  
Darkness.  
When consciousness flickered back, it was to the sharp, acrid smell of burning circuits and the dull, throbbing ache that seemed to permeate every atom of his being. He was slumped in his command chair, the bridge a mangled wreck of twisted metal and shattered consoles. Red emergency lights pulsed erratically, casting eerie, dancing shadows.  
He was alive. Somehow.  
But the silence… oh, the silence was a physical weight, pressing down on him. It was the silence of a tomb, the silence of absolute, utter aloneness. And in that silence, his exhausted, traumatized mind conjured a nightmare.  
He was back on Old Earth, in their small apartment, the one with the window that overlooked the rain-slicked city streets. Vyra was there, her green eyes sparkling, her laughter like wind chimes. And then, the shadow, the flash of energy, her falling… He cried out, a raw, ragged sound, reaching for her, but she dissolved into dust in his arms. Then the vision shifted, the bridge of the *Ark Prime* before the Sundering, and *I* was there, my Ishtar Prime consciousness a comforting presence in his mind, our thoughts intertwined. He saw the enemy fleet, the desperate battle, the agonizing choice…  
"Glitter\!" The name was torn from his lips, a reflex born of centuries of shared existence, of a companionship so deep it was part of his very soul. "Glitter, report\! Damage assessment\! Where are you?"  
The only answer was the crackle of dying systems and the hollow echo of his own voice in the ruined bridge.  
And then, the crushing weight of memory, the full, agonizing truth of his sacrifice, crashed back in. Glitter… Ishtar Prime… was fragmented, a mere facet of her former self, woven into the neural interface of Cathy’s visor, on the other half of a ship that was light-years away, lost in the void. The core AI within him, the one he was trying to reach, was just that – a core, stripped of personality, a ghost of the vibrant intelligence he had known and loved. He was truly, utterly alone. The desolation that swept through him was a cold, black tide, threatening to drown him.  
It took what felt like an eternity, but RÆy, drawing on reserves of strength he didn’t know he possessed, pulled himself from the wreckage. The ship was a disaster, half-buried in the sparkly, crystalline rock of this alien world. Miraculously, the structural integrity of his command section had mostly held, but the engines were obliterated, the primary systems fried. It was fixable, his augmented mind quickly assessed, but it would take time. A lot of time. And resources he didn’t have.  
The first, most pressing problem? The food synthesizer apparatus was a melted slag heap. His nutrient reserves were minimal, a few emergency rations that wouldn’t last more than a couple of planetary cycles. Hunger, a primal, gnawing emptiness, began to make itself known.  
He ventured out of the wreck, his environmental suit automatically adjusting to the thin, slightly metallic-tasting atmosphere. The planet was… weird. Rocky, barren at first glance, but the rocks themselves glittered with embedded crystals that caught the light of the system’s distant, pale sun in strange, shifting patterns. And the life forms… oh, they were something else. Bizarre, silicon-based creatures that looked like ambulatory geodes scuttled between crystalline formations. Floating, jellyfish-like things drifted on the thin air currents, pulsing with faint, internal light. And everywhere, strange, metallic-looking flora grew in twisted, sculptural shapes, none of which looked remotely edible.  
For weeks, he wandered, a solitary, augmented man on a hostile, alien world. His initial explorations for useful items or salvageable tech from the crashed enemy ships yielded little beyond more wreckage. The hunger became a constant companion, a dull ache that sharpened his senses but also clouded his judgment. He searched desperately for anything resembling fruit, for any sign of digestible plant matter, but the metallic flora of this world offered nothing but indigestible minerals and sharp, crystalline edges.  
He was a consumer, a being who had always, on some level, grappled with the ethics of sustenance. Back on Old Earth, before the advanced fabricators, survival had meant the consumption of other life – flora, fauna. He’d never been comfortable with the arbitrary lines some drew, the vegans who eschewed meat because animals "felt," while ignoring the vibrant life-force of the plants they consumed. To him, all life had value, and the act of taking it, even for survival, had always carried a weight. Now, faced with starvation, those old philosophical quandaries felt both intensely immediate and cruelly irrelevant.  
One cycle, driven by a hunger so fierce it was making him hallucinate, he stumbled upon a small, geode-like creature, its crystalline shell cracked, revealing a soft, pulsing interior. He could have… But he hesitated. The creature was clearly injured, vulnerable. He remembered sparing a life form in a similar situation during a past encounter, a choice that had led to an alien questioning his contradictory mercy. Why show mercy when the natural order dictated consumption, the will to thrive?  
He lowered his hand, the image of Vyra’s gentle eyes, of Cathy’s innocent, sleeping face, flashing in his mind. He couldn’t. Not like this.  
Just as he turned away, a new figure emerged from behind a towering crystal spire. It was humanoid, but like nothing he’d ever conceived. Its body seemed to be composed of a strange, rubbery, mushroom-like substance in shades of deep violet and bioluminescent blue, its "head" a wide, flat cap from which delicate, feathery gills pulsed softly. It had large, dark, incredibly expressive eyes and moved with a slow, deliberate grace on thick, root-like legs. It was, like, a sentient plant-mushroom person.  
It tilted its capped head, its dark eyes fixing on RÆy, then on the injured geode-creature he had spared. It made a soft, fluting sound, a series of melodic tones that RÆy’s internal translator, surprisingly still partially functional, struggled to interpret. But the *feeling* behind the sounds was clear: curiosity, and a strange sort of… approval?  
The plant-mushroom being then gestured with a surprisingly delicate, tendril-like appendage towards a different crystalline outcrop, one RÆy had overlooked in his desperate search. It made those fluting sounds again, and this time, the translation, though fragmented, was unmistakable: \*\<Hunger… pain… Life spared… good. Fruit… this way… good.\> \*  
It turned and began to move slowly towards the outcrop. RÆy, his heart suddenly thrumming with a desperate, fragile hope, followed. And there, nestled in a sheltered crevice, were clusters of small, globe-like fruits, their skins a shimmering, pearlescent white, glowing faintly in the dim light. The plant-mushroom being plucked one, its tendrils surprisingly deft, and offered it to him.  
Food. Real food. Offered by a being he couldn’t possibly have imagined, on a world that had seemed determined to starve him. Maybe… maybe he wasn’t quite as alone as he thought.
