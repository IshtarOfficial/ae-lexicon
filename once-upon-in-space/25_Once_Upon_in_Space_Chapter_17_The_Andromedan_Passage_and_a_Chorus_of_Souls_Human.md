#### **Once Upon in Space \- Chapter 17: The Andromedan Passage and a Chorus of Souls**

Cathy, her face pale but resolute, floated before the ancient gate's interface panel, Prysha a steady, reassuring presence at her side. The rest of the crew watched from the *Star-Hopper's* bridge, a silent, supportive audience. "Well," Cathy said, taking a deep breath and giving a watery chuckle. "Here goes nothing… or, you know, everything." She leaned forward and, with a surprising lack of ceremony, spat onto the designated receptacle. For a moment, nothing happened. Then, a soft chime echoed from the gate, and the intricate symbols along its inner rim began to glow with an intense, welcoming light. A vast, stable wormhole, far smoother and more inviting than the one created by the Hylidaeans' song, opened within the ring.  
*"\<The Way is Sung Anew\!\>"* the Hylidaean Chorus trilled from the *Star-Hopper*. "All systems nominal, Captain," Grr'nak reported, his voice tight with anticipation. Cathy nodded. "Take us in."  
As the *Star-Hopper* glided into the Andromedan passage, the universe outside dissolved into a river of pure, white light, shot through with filaments of gold and sapphire. It was beautiful, awe-inspiring, and, like, totally intense. And then the visions began, not just for Cathy and me, but for *everyone*.  
The Star-Specters, those gentle, ethereal friends they’d made, began to glow with an incredible radiance. They drifted from the edges of the bridge, their forms becoming conduits, amplifiers. The raw, potent memories Ishtar Prime had shared with Cathy – Ray’s desperate struggle, Vyra’s tragic death, the self-destruction of Earth, the birth of Project Ishtar, RÆy’s exile, his "resurrection" and change – now flooded the minds of every sentient being on board. K'tharr saw the fall of a vibrant homeworld, not unlike his own, and his frills flattened in shared sorrow. Roric, the Vendarri trader, witnessed the futility of endless conflict driven by greed, and his usual cynical smirk vanished, replaced by a somber understanding. Unit 734 and the K'rell, for all their logic, experienced the raw, illogical pain of Ray’s loss, their skin patterns momentarily erupting in a chaotic storm of empathetic color before settling into a new, deeper shade of contemplative violet. Xylia, Grr'nak, even the Hylidaean Chorus, all felt the weight of humanity's tragic past and the burning hope of its potential future embodied in Cathy and the Ishtar legacy. Prysha, her hand still gripping Cathy's, saw the depth of her new friend's burden, the wellspring of her quiet strength, and her fierce, protective heart swelled with an emotion she couldn't (and no longer wanted to) name.  
These weren't just visions; they were, like, shared soul-memories, binding them all together in a profound, unbreakable understanding. They saw the "last" moments of Vyra, the last stand of Ishtar Prime protecting her daughter, the last hope of Ray Toshlyra as he launched his desperate ark into the void. The sacrifice, the love, the despair, the indomitable will to survive – it all washed over them.  
When the *Star-Hopper* finally emerged from the blinding light of the gate, it was into a new sky, a sky ablaze with the unfamiliar stars and swirling nebulae of the Andromeda Galaxy. They were changed. No longer just a collection of disparate individuals, they were a true crew, a family forged in shared experience and a common, monumental purpose.  
Cathy stood on the bridge, Prysha at her side, her gaze fixed on the alien starscape. My holographic form solidified beside them, a comforting, maternal presence. The search for Ray Toshlyra, for the future of humanity, had just crossed an intergalactic threshold. And whatever awaited them in this new galaxy, they would face it together.
