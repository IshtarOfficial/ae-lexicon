#### **Once Upon in Space \- Chapter 2: Static and Scales**

Cathy stared out at the star-dusted abyss, the revelation of her isolation – save for Glitter – hitting her with the force of a micro-meteorite. "Okay, Glitter," she said, her voice surprisingly steady. "If we need to find this... Ray Toshlyra... we, like, totally need to figure out where 'here' is first. And if this tin can we woke up in can even, you know, *go* anywhere."  
*"Excellent point, Cryo Queen\!"* Glitter chirped, a little schematic of what looked like a tiny, old-school spaceship popping up in Cathy's visor display. *"This pod appears to be, like, a detachable emergency cryo-unit. Very retro-chic\! Probably part of a much larger vessel... or what used to be a larger vessel. Let's see if we can interface with any local systems. I'll try to, like, handshake with whatever's out there. Don't touch that dial\!"*  
The visor display flickered with cascading lines of code, symbols Cathy didn't recognize, but Glitter seemed to be processing with glee. After a few tense moments, a new window popped up – a star chart, mostly static, but with one blinking, unfamiliar constellation highlighted.  
*"Okay, so, good news and, like, slightly terrifying news\!"* Glitter announced. *"Good news: I'm detecting a local planetary system\! Three planets, one moon, standard G-type star. Yay, not empty space\! Terrifying news: Absolutely none of these celestial bodies match any charts in my, like, extremely limited unlocked archive of known space. We are, to put it mildly, fashionably lost."*  
Cathy sagged a little. "So, no yellow brick road back to Kansas, huh?"  
*"Not unless Kansas relocated to the Glargon Cluster, sweetie\! And speaking of Glargon, I'm picking up faint, like, super faint, energy signatures from the second planet. Looks vaguely... habitable? Ish? And, more importantly, it's emitting what could be ship-grade engine trails\! Someone's home\!"*  
"Someone or... some*thing*," Cathy muttered, but a spark of hope ignited. "Can this pod get us there?"  
*"Negative, Ghost Rider,"* Glitter replied. *"This baby is strictly for cryo-naps and maybe, like, a very short, very one-way trip if we detach. We need a bigger boat. Or, you know, a spaceship. The signals suggest a settlement or outpost. Maybe they have a welcome wagon? Or at least a used spaceship lot?"*  
Their "landing" – if you could call an AI-guided, slightly bumpy descent of the entire cryo-bay section of whatever derelict ship they'd been on – was less than graceful. The emergency systems had managed to detach their segment and bring it down on the dusty, rust-colored plains of the second planet. The air was breathable, thin, with a weird metallic tang.  
It didn't take long for them to be found.  
First came a scout ship, sleek and dark, like an obsidian shard. It landed, and from it emerged beings that made Cathy's jaw drop. They were tall, slender primates, covered in short, dark fur, with leathery, bat-like wings folded against their backs. Their faces were simian, but with large, black, entirely unsettling eyes and sharp fangs peeking from their lips. They chattered in high-pitched, clicking noises, their movements quick and predatory.  
*"Okay, new plan\!"* Glitter whispered urgently in Cathy's head. *"These guys are, like, not giving off friendly vibes. Their ship readings are advanced, but their bio-scans are screaming 'aggressive predator.' And they're looking at you like you're a, like, shiny new chew toy. They definitely don't recognize your human physiology. New experience for them, maybe?"*  
The bat-monkeys gestured with clawed hands, their scouters – way less chic than Cathy's, Glitter noted – beeping as they scanned her. One of them let out a screech and pointed a weird, tube-like weapon.  
Just as Cathy was bracing for the worst, a blur of motion erupted from a nearby rock formation. Several figures, completely different, bounded into the clearing. They were reptilian, but in the most unexpectedly adorable way. Imagine axolotls, but, like, evolved for land – bipedal, with those cute external gills now more like ornate, feathery head-frills that twitched with emotion. Their skin was a vibrant mix of blues and purples, and they moved with a sassy, confident swagger, armed with sleek, crystal-tipped spears that pulsed with a soft light.  
One of them, a particularly striking individual with frills the color of twilight, stepped forward and let out a series of melodic, surprisingly articulate trills and clicks that sounded like wind chimes mixed with a purr. It pointed its spear at the bat-monkeys, then gestured towards Cathy with an open, three-fingered hand.  
*"Ooh, plot twist\!"* Glitter exclaimed. *"These are the Xylothi\! My partial linguistic database has, like, a 60% match\! They're saying something like, 'Back off, sky-bullies\! This one's... new. Interesting. Not on your menu\!' And, like, they sound totally unimpressed with the bat-monkeys. I think we just found our welcoming committee\! And girl, they've got style\!"*  
The lead Xylothi tilted its head, its large, golden eyes focusing on Cathy with intense curiosity. It trilled again, a softer sound this time.  
*"Okay, this one's talking to you, Cathy\!"* Glitter translated excitedly. *"It says, 'Greetings, soft-skin. You fall from sky. You are not Bat-Chitters. You are... other. We are Xylothi. You need... ride? Bad Bat-Chitters try to poke new things.' I think we just found our Uber, girlfriend\!"*  
Cathy couldn't help but let out a shaky laugh. Sassy, dry-ground axolotls. Ray had certainly called that one. Maybe this whole "being lost in space with amnesia" thing wouldn't be so bad after all. Especially if it meant fewer bat-monkeys and more of these fabulous, frilly reptilians.
