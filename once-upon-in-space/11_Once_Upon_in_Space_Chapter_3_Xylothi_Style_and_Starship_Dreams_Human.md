#### **Once Upon in Space \- Chapter 3: Xylothi Style and Starship Dreams**

The Xylothi, true to their sassy initial impression, were, like, totally amazing hosts. Their settlement, nestled amongst towering crystal formations that hummed with a faint energy, was a vibrant tapestry of organic-looking structures and glowing, intricate pathways. It was, like, if a coral reef decided to become a futuristic city, but make it desert-chic.  
Cathy, with me, Glitter, acting as a real-time translator and cultural attaché (while also, like, totally fangirling over their aesthetic), quickly began to pick up the Xylothi language – a surprisingly nuanced mix of trills, clicks, and subtle shifts in the iridescence of their frills. The Xylothi, led by the twilight-frilled leader whose name translated roughly to "K'tharr of the Resonant Stone," were endlessly curious about Cathy, the "Star-Fallen Soft-Skin," as they called her.  
*"Okay, Cathy, K'tharr is, like, super impressed with your quick learning\!"* I chirped into her mind one evening as they shared a meal of glowing fungi and synthesized protein paste (surprisingly yummy\!). *"And they're also, like, totally fascinated by ME\! Your 'Voice in the Light Visor.' They think my ability to process their language so fast is, like, super high-level tech. Which, duh, it is\!"*  
It was during a tour of K'tharr's personal workshop – a cavern filled with dazzling crystal-based technology and holographic blueprints – that something major, like, *sproinged* in my databanks. K'tharr was showing Cathy their primary form of energy generation, a complex matrix of resonating crystals.  
*"Whoa, hold the phone\!"* I suddenly exclaimed, my usual bubbly voice tinged with a new kind\_of\_... awe? *"Cathy, the way they're using harmonic resonance in these crystals... it's, like, so similar to a theoretical human energy system I just... remembered\! File Packet Gamma-7-Alpha just unlocked\! It's a whole bunch of human tech schematics – advanced energy generation, gravitic manipulation, even some bio-synthetic material designs\! And honey, it's not just on par with Xylothi tech, some of this stuff is, like, next-level\!"*  
Cathy stared at me, or rather, at the visor. "You remembered? How?"  
*"I think,"* I mused, my internal processors whirring, *"seeing their tech, something about the underlying principles, it, like, jogged a related pathway in my core programming\! It's not the full Ray Toshlyra archive, not by a long shot, but it's... a LOT\! Like, blueprints for compact fusion cores, inertial dampeners that could make a ride smoother than a freshly resurfaced ice rink, and even some foundational notes on, get this, 'bio-neural interface systems'\!"* (Project Ishtar vibes, anyone? Even if *I* couldn't quite place the full significance yet, the term itself felt heavy.)  
The Xylothi, particularly K'tharr, were, like, totally intrigued when Cathy, guided by my newly accessible data, began to share these human technological concepts. Their society was advanced, but they had focused more on crystalline energy systems and biological engineering. Human tech, with its emphasis on miniaturization, robust mechanical systems, and different approaches to physics, was a revelation.  
And that’s when the big idea sparked.  
*"K'tharr,"* Cathy said one day, with me translating and adding helpful visual aids via her visor, *"what if we combined our knowledge? Your understanding of resonant crystal power and organic construction, with... Glitter's... uh, human knowledge of gravitic drives and long-range sensor systems? We need a ship, a proper deep-space vessel. What if we built one together?"*  
K'tharr’s frills vibrated with excitement, their golden eyes gleaming. The idea of a collaborative starship project, a fusion of two distinct technological philosophies, was, like, too good to pass up.  
And so, "Project Star-Hopper," as Glitter cheekily named it, began. The design that emerged was, like, totally iconic: a sleek, saucer-shaped vessel, its hull a shimmering, Xylothi-grown bio-alloy, interwoven with human-designed sensor arrays. At its heart, a hybrid crystal-fusion engine promised incredible range and speed. It was, like, a classic UFO, but with Xylothi flair and human ingenuity.  
Of course, nothing this awesome goes unnoticed. The Bat-Chitters, those cranky, winged primates, had clearly been keeping tabs. During a crucial phase of engine calibration, a squadron of their dark, jagged ships descended, weapons firing.  
*"Incoming party crashers\!"* I announced, my voice all business now. *"Hostile signatures, Bat-Chitters, and they look, like, super intent on stealing our sparkly new toys\!"*  
K'tharr let out a battle trill, and the Xylothi warriors, armed with their crystal spears and newly upgraded energy shields (a little something I helped them whip up from my recalled data), moved to defend the construction site. Cathy, surprisingly, found herself not cowering but, like, totally focused. The visor flashed with tactical data I was feeding her, and a strange, calm certainty settled over her. She pointed out a structural weakness in the lead Bat-Chitter ship to K'tharr, a suggestion based on some obscure engineering principle I'd just recalled.  
K'tharr, trusting the "Voice in the Light," directed their fire. The Bat-Chitter ship, hit precisely where Cathy (and I\!) had indicated, spiraled out of control. The other hostiles, seeing their leader disabled and facing determined, technologically enhanced Xylothi resistance, screeched in frustration and retreated. No tech was stolen, no intel compromised. Just a bunch of very miffed bat-monkeys.  
Later, as the Xylothi celebrated their victory and the near-completion of the Star-Hopper, K'tharr placed a three-fingered hand on Cathy’s shoulder. Their frills glowed with respect.  
*"The Star-Fallen has a warrior's mind as well as a builder's,"* K'tharr trilled, translated by me with a proud little flourish. *"You see patterns others miss. Perhaps this 'Ray Toshlyra' who made your Voice also made you... for more than just remembering."*  
Cathy looked up at the nearly finished saucer, sleek and silver under the alien sky. For the first time, she felt a sense of purpose, a stirring of something more than just survival. And somewhere, locked deep within me, the name "Project Ishtar" pulsed, waiting for its moment.
