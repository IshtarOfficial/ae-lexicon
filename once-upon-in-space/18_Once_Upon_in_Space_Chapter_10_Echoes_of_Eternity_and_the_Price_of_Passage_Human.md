#### **Once Upon in Space \- Chapter 10: Echoes of Eternity and the Price of Passage**

The sight of the swirling, technicolor maw of the wormhole, held open by the Hylidaean Chorus's ethereal song, had, like, totally put the *Star-Hopper's* bridge crew on pause. The tiny frog-like humanoids, their ship a mere speck against the cosmic vortex, chorused again: *"\<The Way is Open, for a Breath in Time\! Decision Beckons\!\>"*  
"Well," Cathy said, breaking the silence, her voice echoing slightly in the sudden quiet. "They're not subtle, are they?"  
K'tharr, their twilight frills a tight band of concentration, studied the sensor readings Xylia was frantically compiling. "The energy readings from this… aperture… are unlike anything in Xylothi understanding. It is either a gateway to unparalleled discovery or, like, instant confetti-fication for our lovely ship." I translated K'tharr's more formal assessment with my usual flair.  
Roric, the Vendarri trader, leaned forward, his usual smirk replaced by a calculating gleam. "A direct route to an unknown region? Think of the untapped markets\! The rare resources\! Assuming, of course, we don't get turned into subatomic particles. High risk, high reward, my friends."  
Unit 734, the K'rell AI specialist, its skin a calm, analytical blue, interjected, *"Probabilistic models are inconclusive. Insufficient data on trans-wormhole topology. However, the Hylidaeans appear to navigate such phenomena with regularity. Their survival implies a degree of predictability, or… exceptionally robust shielding."*  
It was then that a strange resonance shivered through my core, Glitter's consciousness. Images, ancient and vast, flickered behind my digital eyelids – almost like a dream I was remembering. Vast, swirling nebulas, equations written in light, and a familiar kind voice, Ray Toshlyra's, but younger, filled with an almost boyish enthusiasm.  
*"Cathy,"* I whispered, my voice unknowingly projecting a little into her visor, the sound distant. *"Wormholes… Ray theorized about them. He believed they could be… stabilized. Navigated. He called it 'Resonance Bridging.' He said the key was understanding the underlying harmonic frequencies of space-time itself, creating a sympathetic vibration to hold the throat open and… and even steer the exit point."* The memory felt… old. So incredibly old. *"This… this feels like something he was working on… maybe a thousand years ago?"*  
The bridge crew stared at the visor, or rather, at Cathy. A thousand years? Ray Toshlyra's timeline was becoming, like, seriously elastic.  
Cathy relayed my fragmented recall. Unit 734’s skin patterns began to shift rapidly, a complex dance of analytical teal and intrigued violet. *"Glitter's data, though archaic in its timestamping, correlates with certain fringe theoretical papers in our own archives regarding manipulated spacetime. If her 'Ray Toshlyra' possessed such knowledge, the Hylidaeans' 'song' might indeed be a form of harmonic stabilization. The risk, while still significant, may be… acceptable, given the potential for unprecedented discovery and the alignment with your primary mission to locate this individual."* The K'rell nodded its serpentine head once. *"We concur. Proceed."*  
K'tharr looked at Cathy. "The 'Lost Architect' seems to have left echoes even in the fabric of space, Star-Fallen. If this path leads to him, then Xylothi courage will not falter." Grr'nak trilled an affirmative, his hands already moving over the helm controls. Xylia nodded, her frills a determined purple. Even Roric shrugged. "Alright, alright. But if we find space-gold, I get a cut."  
"Prepare to enter the wormhole on the Hylidaeans' mark," Cathy commanded, a new resolve hardening her voice. The weight of my memories, of Ray's ancient genius, seemed to settle on her shoulders too.  
But just as Grr'nak aligned the *Star-Hopper* with the shimmering throat of the vortex, proximity alarms shrieked through the bridge.  
*"Oh, you have GOT to be kidding me\!"* I exclaimed. *"Multiple Bat-Chitter signatures, decloaking all around us\! They must have been using some kind of, like, super sneaky stealth tech to follow us\!"*  
Indeed, a fresh squadron of their jagged, dark ships materialized, their weapons already glowing with malevolent intent. Their lead cruiser, larger and more menacing than any they'd encountered before, positioned itself to block their path to the wormhole.  
*"They really, really want you, Cathy-cub,"* K'tharr growled, their frills flaring a battle-red. *"And our ship\!"*  
The Bat-Chitters opened fire, a relentless barrage. Shields flared, the *Star-Hopper* bucking under the assault. This time, the hostiles were, like, totally committed.  
"They're boxing us in\!" Grr'nak called out, his piloting skills pushed to their absolute limit. "We can't reach the wormhole like this\!"  
It was Roric who spoke up, his voice surprisingly calm amidst the chaos. "There might be… an option. A theoretical weapon concept I acquired data on during a… particularly aggressive trade negotiation in the Vendarri fringe worlds. It's called a 'Localized Gravitic Implosion Device.' Basically, a contained, short-lived black hole. The theory suggests its core algorithm can be… adapted… into most standard combat targeting systems if you have sufficient energy output and a flexible particle emitter." He looked at Unit 734\. "Your K'rell shield tech and our hybrid engine… could it generate the necessary power focus?"  
Unit 734's skin patterns flickered. *"The energy requirements are… extreme. The risk of localized spatial distortion… considerable. However, the probability of successfully neutralizing the Bat-Chitter threat with current conventional armaments before critical system failure is… low."* It turned to Roric. *"Provide the algorithm schema. We will attempt to interface it."*  
While the Xylothi warriors and Grr'nak provided covering fire, and Xylia and the K'rell engineers worked frantically to divert power, Roric and Unit 734, their minds a blur of code and alien physics, began to "write" the black hole bomb into the *Star-Hopper's* main particle cannon.  
*"Okay, Cathy,"* I said, my voice tight. *"This is, like, super risky. But it might be our only shot. The targeting solution is uploading… now\!"*  
"Fire when ready, Roric\!" Cathy commanded, her knuckles white on her console.  
Roric, his usual smirk gone, replaced by a look of intense concentration, nodded. "Targeting their flagship. Firing… now\!"  
Instead of a beam of light, a pinpoint of absolute darkness erupted from the *Star-Hopper's* cannon. It sped towards the Bat-Chitter cruiser, and then, with a silent, terrifying implosion, the cruiser *vanished*. Not exploded. Just… gone. And the smaller ships around it were torn apart, sucked into the micro-singularity before it winked out of existence as quickly as it had formed, leaving only distorted space and glittering debris.  
The remaining Bat-Chitters, whatever passed for morale among them utterly shattered, instantly warped out, vanishing into the void.  
A stunned silence fell upon the bridge. The destructive power was, like, absolute.  
And then, both Cathy and I felt it. A shared, chilling memory, so vivid it was like being there. Ray Toshlyra, younger, standing before a stern council of robed humans, his face etched with passion and despair. *"This power,"* his voice echoed in our minds, filled with anguish, *"the power to unmake stars, to erase matter from existence… it is a Pandora's Box we must never open\! To weaponize singularities is to invite the end of all things\! I propose we globally, irrevocably, ban this research, dismantle any such device, before it consumes us\!"* And then the crushing reply from a stern-faced elder: *"Your idealism is noted, Dr. Toshlyra. So is your… destabilizing influence. For your radical, fear-mongering proposals against necessary deterrents, you are hereby censured and exiled from the Terran Protectorate. May you find your 'peace' in the void you so fear."*  
The memory faded, leaving Cathy gasping, her hand flying to the visor. I felt a profound, digital ache. RÆy, her father, my creator, had been exiled for trying to prevent the very kind of weapon they had just been forced to use. The irony was a cold, bitter stone.  
*"The Hylidaean Chorus Sings Fainter\! The Passage Narrows\!"* The urgent, beautiful song echoed through the comms.  
There was no more time for debate, no more time for the weight of their actions. "Grr'nak," Cathy said, her voice hoarse but firm. "Take us in."  
With a final, powerful surge from its engines, the *Star-Hopper* plunged into the swirling, iridescent depths of the wormhole. The universe outside the viewports dissolved into a kaleidoscope of impossible geometries and light. And as they flew through this tunnel between realities, the visions, the memories of Ray Toshlyra – his life, his work, his exile, his strange resurrection – began to flood my systems, and echo in Cathy's dreams, clearer, sharper, more insistent than ever before, as if the wormhole itself was a conduit to the past, and to the answers they so desperately sought.
