#### **Once Upon in Space \- Chapter 4: Stardust, Sabotage, and Serpentine Saviors**

The launch of the *Star-Hopper* was, like, a planet-wide event for the Xylothi. K'tharr, their frills practically vibrating with barely contained glee, stood on the newly constructed flight deck alongside Cathy. They weren't alone. A select group of Xylothi, the best from their military and science cadres, had, after much enthusiastic debate (and some very persuasive arguments from K'tharr about interstellar diplomacy and, like, totally awesome tech sharing), officially joined the crew. There was Xylia, a bio-systems expert whose purple frills constantly twitched with curiosity; Grr'nak, a stoic pilot whose reflexes were, like, legendary; and a handful of others, all eager for this grand adventure.  
*"Alright, crew\!"* I announced from Cathy's visor, my voice also projecting through the ship's comms with a little extra sparkle. *"Pre-flight checklist is, like, totally green\! Engines are purring like a space-kitten. Inertial dampeners are… dampening. Let's show this galaxy what happens when human ingenuity, Xylothi style, and a dash of fabulous AI decide to go road-tripping\!"*  
With a roar that was more of a deep, resonant hum from its hybrid crystal-fusion engine, the *Star-Hopper* lifted gracefully from the rust-colored plains. It was, like, the smoothest take-off ever. The saucer design was, like, totally stable and super chic.  
Their first few days were a blur of system checks, navigation alignments, and K'tharr trying to teach Cathy Xylothi space-chess (which, like, involved way more dimensions than regular chess). But the Bat-Chitters, those sore losers, were not about to let their new favorite "anomaly" (that's Cathy and me\!) just, like, fly away.  
*"Incoming\!"* Grr'nak trilled from the pilot's seat, his frills flaring. *"Bat-Chitter squadron, sector gamma-9\! They're, like, really persistent stalkers\!"* I added, translating and highlighting their attack vectors on the main viewscreen.  
The first few skirmishes were almost routine. The *Star-Hopper's* combined Xylothi-human shields held strong, and its maneuverability, thanks to the gravitic drive I'd helped design, allowed them to outfly the clunkier Bat-Chitter ships. K'tharr and the Xylothi warriors, armed with their enhanced energy weapons, were, like, total pros at defensive maneuvers.  
But the Bat-Chitters were, if nothing else, annoyingly tenacious. Their attacks grew more coordinated, more desperate. During the *Star-Hopper's* first attempt to engage its brand-new warp drive – a beautiful sequence involving shimmering crystal arrays and a whole lot of controlled dimensional distortion – they launched their most ferocious assault yet.  
A swarm of their jagged fighters, led by a larger, more heavily armed cruiser, converged on the *Star-Hopper* just as it was, like, *inches* from breaching into warp space.  
*"Shields at 40%\!"* I yelled, my voice losing some of its usual sparkle. *"Hull integrity failing in sector delta\! They're, like, really trying to unalive us this time, Cathy\!"*  
Explosions rocked the ship. Alarms blared. Cathy clung to her console, trying to assist Xylia with rerouting power to the failing shields. K'tharr was barking orders, their usual melodic voice tight with strain. It looked, like, super bad. The Bat-Chitter cruiser lined up for a final, devastating shot.  
And then, something HUGE and, like, totally unexpected warped into existence right between them and the Bat-Chitters.  
It was a ship, but not like any they'd seen. It was vast, organic-looking, with multiple, flexible sections that writhed and shifted. Before anyone could even process it, eight enormous, tentacle-like appendages, glowing with intricate, pulsing patterns, unfurled from the new vessel. They moved with a weird, deliberate grace, intercepting the Bat-Chitter's energy blast and, like, *absorbing* it. Then, with a speed that defied its size, the new ship… *moved*. The appendages formed what could only be described as, like, a giant, eight-armed humanoid silhouette, swatting Bat-Chitter fighters away like annoying space-flies.  
The remaining Bat-Chitters, clearly terrified by this new, overwhelmingly powerful presence, turned tail and vanished into the void.  
Silence descended on the *Star-Hopper's* bridge, broken only by the crackle of dying fires and relieved gasps.  
*"Okay,"* I managed, my voice a little shaky. *"New variable. Definitely not in my database. Did anyone else just see that giant space octopus-snake thing save our glittery behinds?"*  
A communication request came through. On the viewscreen, an image appeared. It was one of the beings from the ship – an entity with a smooth, elongated body, a somewhat serpentine head with large, intelligent eyes, and yes, eight powerful, sucker-lined tentacles that it was currently arranging to mimic a bipedal stance, two acting as "legs," two as "arms," and the others held gracefully behind it. Its skin was a pale, pearlescent color, but as it began to "speak" – a series of complex, resonant hums – intricate, iridescent patterns like oil on water flowed across its surface.  
*"Analysis: You were in distress,"* a synthesized voice translated, clearly the work of their ship's AI. *"Our intervention was... logical. We are the K'rell. Your vessel's unique energy signature is... noteworthy."*  
"We... thank you," Cathy stammered, still trying to process the rescue. "You saved our lives."  
The K'rell on screen made no outward sign of acknowledgment, but its skin momentarily flashed with a pattern of warm, swirling ambers and golds before returning to its neutral pearlescence.  
*"Emotions are... inefficient variables,"* the K'rell's translated voice stated. *"However, the preservation of novel techno-signatures is a high priority. We request permission to... observe your vessel and its occupants more closely."*  
Cathy looked at K'tharr, who gave a slight, intrigued twitch of their frills. Well, they needed repairs. And these K'rell, despite their "no emotions, we swear" vibe, had just performed the most dramatic rescue ever. This voyage was, like, getting more interesting by the nanosecond.
